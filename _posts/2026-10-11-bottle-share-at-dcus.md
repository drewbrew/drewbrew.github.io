Over the past few years at DCUS, I've inadvertently started something of a tradition. People routinely get together and hang out in the lobby for far later than we should, but it's easy to let the time fly by when you're catching up with old friends and meeting new friends.

This has nothing to do with my role as an organizer or board member, for the record.

**Content warning**: this will frequently mention alcohol but there's nothing stopping you from taking these lessons and applying them to anything else you can share like sweets.

## A little history

Here in Huntsville, where I have lived for quite a few years now, we have a group of craft beer enthusiasts that isn't as active as it used to be, but we get together for a bottle share at least once a year where people will bring things they've acquired over their travels and share things that we can't normally get locally. It's a great way to try new styles and also enjoy small pours of things you would never buy a full bottle/pint of, let alone a six-pack. For me, those are sours and pastry stouts, but everyone has their own tastes.

## DCUS 2023-24

I drove to DjangoCon US 2023 and 2024 so I could stop at my grandparents' house near Asheville on the way. This gave me the advantage of more cargo room than I could ask for, so I brought both my road and gravel bikes as well as a cooler featuring a few beers I've had aging for a while.

The first year, it was a small thing: me, Tim Schilling, and two other people, so it was a small enough group that we just hung out in my hotel room. It was a lot of fun and we had some excellent conversations.

Word broke out a little bit, and so for 2024, we ended up taking up a table in the Marriott lobby. It was still a small group (5-6 people) but it was cool introducing people to things they couldn't otherwise enjoy.

## DCUS 2025

In 2025 in Chicago, I packed a few selections in my checked bag and flew to Chicago with them. The crowd ended up getting a little bigger (about 10 people hanging out, although only 5-6 of us were partaking in the tastings). My favorite parts was probably introducing Kattni to a few styles she would never have otherwise tried and she ended up liking them and Rachell's wife giving me a Korean soda to try.

## DCUS 2026

In 2026, I was able to do the same packing as well as supplementing with a few local things I picked up from a bottle shop in Chicago. The crowd got massive over the two nights I had this. Paulo got a [selfie](https://www.paulox.net/2026/08/31/my-djangocon-us-2026/#2026-08-26) with I think 14 of us in the group. We got close to 20 people at one point. Lots of conversations flowed: discussions about beer, life, and everything else. I ended up going to bed "early" (around 11-11:30 PM) because I had to get up for bike rides the next morning.  It's probably my favorite part of after-hours socializing at DCUS now.

## How to set one up at your conference

### Prerequisites
1. Either fly domestic or take a car/bus/train to the conference. When flying into the US, you're limited to 1 liter of alcohol per person, so it's a lot easier to do this domestically.
2. Bring some extra cups to share along with dish soap (for washing afterwards). I bring 3 Silipint cups and 2 stainless steel cups.
3. If you have people who you know will be joining you, message them before the conference and encourage them to bring a few cans to share.

### Packing tips
1. Stick to cans. Bottles are more fragile and heavier, both of which are major negatives when flying. (NOTE: yes, I know the name bottle share is a misnomer...)
2. Pack 2-4 cans into a gallon ziploc to provide a little insurance in case a leak occurs (I haven't had one yet...)
3. Wrap your clothes tightly around each ziploc to provide the most secure packing possible.

### At the conference
1. Pick a location easily accessible to everyone that is away from the hotel's bar/restaurant (if it has one). Ideally it'll be an area that can easily be expanded by people bringing in chairs, etc.
2. Post on the conference's chat platform (Discord/Slack/etc.) announcing when you're planning to be there. Don't be overzealous about it. Lots of people have reasons for not drinking (or limiting their intake), and that's to be respected completely.
3. Do your homework: have suggestions for where people can go to pick up beverages to share within walking distance of the venue. For DCUS 2027, your best bet is probably going to be [Friendly Liquor Store](https://maps.app.goo.gl/E9xrU2Vv16uZePkCA), about a 5-block walk from the hotel.
4. Work in a palate-friendly progression of styles: start with lighter lagers and non-hop-forward ales, then work your way to darker beers like stouts and porters, then finish up with palate wreckers like sours and IPAs.
5. If possible, have a story to tell about each beer you're pouring. Don't make it a big speech, but a 1-2 sentence explanation of why this beer or brewery is special helps.
6. Keep things light and cheery: nobody is here to get wasted. We're here to try new things and have a great time.

## Wrap-up

Have fun, enjoy the company, and maybe you'll start a new tradition at your conference too! (It's also considerably cheaper than hotel bar tabs)
